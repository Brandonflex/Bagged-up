01Point of view

Three planes.
One point of view.

Type sits behind the subject. The bag holds the middle plane. The action sits above both. That gives the screen a little depth without dressing it up for effect. The approved City Collectors palette and Modern Atelier restraint stay in place. Product details remain easy to find.

01 / Signature studies

A signature, not a symbol.

The linked handles have warmth, but on their own they can read as a generic bag icon. Keep that gesture as a quiet stitch or pattern. The primary mark brings a custom B/U ligature into one folded line. The full wordmark names Bagged Up on first contact. The monogram carries the memory after that.

07 / Voice and writing

A person,
not a pitch.

Premium does not need a pile of grand adjectives. It comes through in what we notice, what we can prove and what we leave out.

MeasuredObservantWarmDirect
The line we skip

“Discover our exquisite collection of timeless luxury handbags.”

Big promise. No detail. Could belong to anyone.
The Bagged Up voice

“A considered edit, chosen for shape and finish. We inspect every bag before dispatch.”

Specific, honest and easy to picture.
01
Start with what you can see.

Name the shape, clasp, colour or strap. Do not invent a material or a story.

02
Pair feeling with a useful fact.

One sensory detail, then the size, fit, price or delivery information people need.

03
Talk to one person.

Use “you” and “we” naturally. Keep the sentence the way you would say it in a good shop.

04
Make the next step obvious.

“Shop the city edit.” “See the bag.” “Ask us about this style.” No mystery arrows.

A practical promise, in our voice

“We check each bag before dispatch. You can pay when it arrives.”

The research behind the wording

Nielsen Norman Group recommends choosing a deliberate tone, then checking how real people read it. Its web-writing research favours concise, scannable and objective copy over hype. Baymard's product-page research adds a useful test: descriptions should answer real questions and work with the photos, especially around size and scale. The Office for National Statistics guidance reinforces plain words, active voice and consistent terms.
