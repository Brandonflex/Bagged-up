01Point of view

City roots.
Atelier finish.

The City Collectors route brings the point of view. Modern Atelier brings the edit and the finish. Mineral paper and forest ink set a grounded base. Clay adds a confident signal; brass stays on small details. Spacious layouts and precise product information keep the store easy to shop.

01 / Logo studies

A mark with a reason to exist.

A pair of linked handles is the starting sketch. It nods to the shape of a bag without drawing one literally. The wordmark stays readable first; the symbol earns its place only if it holds up at small sizes.

Primary / light

The two-tone serif wordmark keeps a human edge. The handle mark adds one ownable detail.

02 / Color system

Grounded first.
Distinctive by degrees.

The warm base carries product photography. Forest ink does the work of structure and action. Clay is the readable accent; marigold is reserved for metallic-feeling details and small highlights.

03 / Typography

Soft expression.
Clear decisions.

The working pair uses the locally hosted Cormorant Garamond and Jost files already in the project. Their roles become more deliberate here. A second typeface round can compare alternatives before the identity is locked.

04 / Image direction

Let the bag lead.
Let the city show.

Use lived-in light, architecture and texture to give the edit a sense of place. Keep the bag visible and true to the item for sale. Avoid costume-like local signifiers and generic luxury props.

01 / ContextShow believable settings and materials, not status props.
02 / Product truthDo not hide color, scale or construction behind heavy grading.
03 / RhythmAlternate close product views with a few wider campaign frames.

06 / Brand in use

One system.
Different scales.

The same loop, colors and type should work on a phone screen, a delivery note and a simple paper wrap. Keep materials and finishes practical to source.

Social / campaign tile

Review checkpoint

Before this becomes the brand.

This is the first full concept pass. It does not change the live logo, theme, fonts or checkout. The next round should resolve the mark and accent balance before implementation.

  1. Does the linked-handle mark feel ownable, or should the wordmark stand alone?
  2. Should clay stay this restrained, or move closer to a stronger rust accent?
  3. Does the image direction feel recognizably local without relying on visual shortcuts?
  4. Does the campaign energy feel distinctive while product names, prices and shopping actions stay easy to scan?
