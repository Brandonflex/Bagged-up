2The 60-second version

If you only read one section, read this one.

  1. Go to app.netlify.com/drop
  2. Drag Bagged-Up-website.zip onto the big dashed box on that page. Let go.
  3. Wait about a minute. You'll get a live website at an address like sparkly-cupcake-12345.netlify.app
  4. Sign up free with your email address when it asks, so the site is tied to you and doesn't disappear.
That's it

You now own a working copy of the shop. No credit card, no GitHub account, no software to install. Netlify's free plan is more than enough for a shop this size.

3Getting it online, properly

The quick version above works, but here's the version where you keep it.

Option A — Netlify Drop (recommended, easiest)

  1. Open app.netlify.com/drop in Chrome, Safari or Edge.
  2. Drag the zip file onto the page.
  3. When it finishes, click "Claim site" (or similar) and create a free account with your email.
  4. Bookmark app.netlify.com — that's your dashboard, where your site lives from now on.

Option B — Cloudflare Pages (if you'd rather stay on Cloudflare)

  1. Log in at dash.cloudflare.com
  2. Go to Workers & Pages → Create → Pages → Upload assets.
  3. Give it a name (e.g. bagged-up) and drag in the zip file.
  4. Click Deploy.
Heads up

Both options above upload a folder directly — that's the whole point. Do not choose the "Connect to Git" or "Import from GitHub" option on either site. That's the path that needs a developer, and it's the one thing that will make this confusing. You want the plain upload / drag-and-drop option every time.

Picking a nicer address

Both hosts give you a free web address. On Netlify you can change it: Site configuration → Change site name. Something like bagged-up.netlify.app is fine and free.

4Using your own domain name

If you have a domain like baggedup.co.ke, this is how it points at the new site.

A domain name (the address) and hosting (where the files live) are two separate things you can buy from two different companies. You're moving the hosting; the domain stays where you bought it.

The short version: this is the one step worth a 10-minute phone call with whoever built the site. It involves logging into where you bought the domain and changing a setting called nameservers or DNS records. Get it slightly wrong and the site goes offline for a few hours — annoying, but not permanent and never destructive.

What your developer needs from you

Send them these three things and they can usually do it themselves in five minutes:

  • Where you bought the domain (e.g. Kenya Web Experts, Truehost, Safaricom, Namecheap, GoDaddy)
  • The username and password for that account
  • The address of your new Netlify or Cloudflare site
Do not let the domain expire

Domains are rented yearly, not bought once. If the renewal lapses, the website vanishes from the internet even though the files are fine. Put a reminder in your phone a month before it renews. This is the single most common way a small site "breaks".

There is no card payment on the site. Customers browse, add bags to a cart, and when they check out the site hands them over to WhatsApp with a ready-written message. You then talk to them, agree on payment (M-Pesa, bank transfer, etc.) and arrange delivery. That's deliberate — it suits how people actually buy in Kenya, and it means no payment fees and no chargeback risk.

Orders land on WhatsApp — check it daily

Every "Ask about this bag" button, every cart checkout and every contact form sends the customer to WhatsApp number 254113599345. If that number isn't one you check constantly, orders will sit unread. If you're taking over with a different number, see the warning in section 6 — it's a job for your developer, not a find-and-replace.

6Making changes

What you can safely do yourself, and what to send back to your developer.

✅ Safe to do yourself

Upload a new version of the site. Your developer sends you a fresh Bagged-Up-website.zip. In your Netlify dashboard, go to Deploys, and simply drag the new zip onto the page. It replaces the old one in about a minute. You can do this as often as you like, and you can't break anything permanently — every deploy is kept in a list, and you can roll back to any earlier one with one click (Publish deploy on an older entry).

⚠️ Send these to your developer

These are all doable, they just need someone with the right tools. A message like "change the Pearl Accent bag from 1,199 to 1,350" is a five-minute job and should cost you nothing if you're on good terms.

  • Change a price — say the bag name and the new price
  • Mark something sold out — say the bag name
  • Add a new bag — send the name, price, category, and the photos
  • Change delivery fees — tell them the new amounts
  • Edit the About / FAQ / Delivery pages — send the new wording

Why you shouldn't do these yourself: a single price appears in about seven different places per product — the shop page, the product page, the WhatsApp message, Google's preview text, and the "you may also like" cards on other bags. Change one and miss another and you get a shop showing two different prices for the same bag. It's not hard, it's just easy to get wrong by hand.

🚫 Never hand-edit these

Do not open the website files in Notepad or TextEdit and change things yourself. Specifically:

  • Don't try to change the WhatsApp number by hand. It's baked into 288 links across the site. Miss a few and some buttons silently message the old number. There's a script for this — ask your developer, it takes them two minutes.
  • Don't rename or move any file inside the zip. The pages reference each other by exact path; one rename and a page 404s.
  • Don't edit assets/js/ or the .css files. That's the layout and behaviour of the whole site.

8Keeping it safe

Two minutes now saves a lot of pain later.

  • Copy this whole folder to Google Drive or Dropbox. Then it survives a lost or stolen laptop.
  • Email yourself a copy of the zip. A second backup costs nothing.
  • Save your Netlify/Cloudflare login in a password manager (or write it down somewhere safe). Losing this is the annoying one to recover.
  • Put the domain renewal date in your phone calendar with a reminder a month early.

9Handover checklist

Tick these off together so nobody's guessing later.

  • Site is live at the new address and looks right
  • Old Vercel site is switched off (so Google doesn't see two copies of the same shop)
  • Domain points at the new site, if there is one
  • WhatsApp number on the site is one the new owner actually checks
  • New owner has the Netlify/Cloudflare login
  • New owner has this folder backed up in two places
  • New owner knows who to message for changes

👷 For the developer only — new owner, you can skip this

Two commands cover every change the new owner is likely to ask for. Run them from the project folder, then send over a fresh zip from handover/.

Rebuild the whole handover pack (site + price list + re-dates this guide):

npm run handover

Change the WhatsApp number everywhere — 400+ places across the pre-rendered pages, in both 254XXXXXXXXX and +254 XXX XXX XXX form:

npm run set-contact -- 0712345678

Then sanity-check and rebuild:

npm run verify && npm run handover

Price changes are the one thing with no script yet: a price appears in assets/js/data.js, the card in shop.html, and roughly seven spots in shop/<slug>.html (price tag, meta description, og:description, two WhatsApp links, and the "you may also like" cards on other pages). Until that's scripted, treat price edits as a manual job.
